How Image Tokens Learn the Language of Radiology

What 2,400 X-ray studies reveal about how models represent clinical findings

Aakash Mishra, Arjun Karpur

Epsilon Health Research · Oct 14, 2026

Read the postCite

We read the image tokens of our report-drafting model through its own clinical vocabulary and with linear probes. Deeper in the language model, they come to point at the right findings, with no direct supervision on the image tokens.

Abstract

Before a vision-language model writes a single word, it embeds each input image as a sequence of image tokens that have not yet seen any text. Do these tokens carry information that a radiologist would use, and how does the language model handle them?

In this post, we read the hidden states of image tokens from a radiology report-drafting VLM at increasing layer depths, and investigate them through the model's own clinical vocabulary and with linear probes. We observe that clinically useful information is largely present within hidden states of the input and early layers of the language model. Interestingly, as the image tokens are processed by the causal language decoder in later layers, their representations increasingly align with the model's own words for the findings present, despite no direct supervision on the image tokens.
